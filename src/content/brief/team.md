# The team

The roles come from an incident warroom, where the same five jobs decide whether a response is
believable. Research has the same shape: someone runs the thing, someone reads the numbers, someone
keeps the record, someone tries to break the claim, and someone watches without touching anything.

| Role | Owns | Refuses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinator | the run: scenario, seed, cadence | to interpret the result |
| Analyst | the reading: what the numbers did | to run the scenario they analyse |
| Archivist | provenance: version, seed, commit, file | to edit a result after publication |
| Skeptic | the counter-case: what else explains it | to sign off on their own objection |
| Observer | read-only access and the audit trail | to change anything |

## Why the split

The coordinator who also analyses will find what they expected. The analyst who also archives will
quietly fix a bad run. The skeptic who also coordinates will approve their own work. Each separation
exists because the combined version has a known failure, not because the team is large enough to
need it.

## One person, five hats

A small team wears several. The rule is not that the roles are different people; it is that the
**hat is declared**. A review note that says "skeptic: same person as coordinator" is honest. One
that does not say so is a review that did not happen.
